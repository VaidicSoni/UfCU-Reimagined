Are credit unions better than banks?
Uncover the vital differences and secure your savings with the right choice today.
Updated September 4, 2026
Published September 3, 2026
Be a Wise Consumer
Credit unions can offer competitive rates, fewer fees, personal guidance, and Member ownership. Banks may be a better fit when you need extensive national access, global services, or highly specialized technology.
Money should support your goals, not create more stress. Yet many financial institutions offer similar
checking accounts
, savings accounts, cards, and loans. The most significant differences often appear in ownership, pricing, access, and service.
Banks usually operate to generate profits for shareholders. Credit unions are not-for-profit financial cooperatives owned by their Members. Their structure can affect how much you earn on savings, how much you pay to borrow, and how you are treated when you need support.
Understanding the differences can help you choose the right credit union or bank for your needs. Here is what Austin, TX, residents should review before making a decision.
Are Credit Unions Safer Than Banks?
Federally insured credit unions and federally insured banks offer similar levels of deposit protection. Banks generally receive coverage through the Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation, or FDIC.
Federally insured credit unions receive coverage through the National Credit Union Share Insurance Fund. The National Credit Union Administration, or NCUA, manages the fund.
Eligible deposits
are generally
insured up to $250,000
per depositor or Member, per insured institution, for each account ownership category. Individual, joint, retirement, trust, and business accounts may receive separate coverage when all requirements are met.
UFCU states that Member funds receive federal share insurance up to applicable limits. Their headquarters are located at UFCU Plaza on North MoPac Expressway in Austin, Texas.
Anyone focused on safe banking should confirm that an institution has federal insurance. You should also learn how account ownership and beneficiaries can affect your total protection.
Federal insurance does not prevent fraud or identity theft. Strong security practices still matter. Use unique passwords, transaction alerts, multi-factor authentication, and card controls whenever available.
Are there any Disadvantages to Credit Unions?
Membership requirements can add an extra step. Eligibility may depend on where you live, work, study, worship, or belong to an approved organization.
Some smaller credit unions may also have fewer physical locations than large national banks. Advanced international services, extensive investment platforms, or specialized commercial tools may not always be available.
Modern technology has reduced many of these disadvantages. Shared branches, fee-free ATM networks, mobile deposits, digital payments, and online support have closed many access gaps. UFCU also offers local locations and a Mobile Branch that can bring selected services and educational resources into the community.
Availability can still vary between institutions. Compare actual features instead of assuming every credit union has limited access.
Ask whether the credit union offers:
Convenient branches and ATMs
Mobile check deposits
Digital card controls
Online bill payment
Fast Member support
Services that match your long-term goals
A credit union may provide everything you need. Another institution may lack a service that matters to you. A direct comparison can help you avoid surprises.
How Ownership Changes Your Experience
Ownership is one of the biggest differences between a credit union and bank. Investors own banks. Members own credit unions.
Members often elect a volunteer board of directors and receive an equal vote. Credit unions do not have outside shareholders demanding higher returns.
Earnings can instead support
:
Competitive rates
Reduced fees
Improved services
Community programs
Common credit union benefits may include:
Competitive savings and certificate yields
Lower rates on certain loans and credit cards
Fewer or lower service fees
Personalized financial guidance
Local decision-making
Financial education
and community support
Member ownership can also influence the service experience. Credit unions are designed to build long-term relationships rather than focus only on individual transactions.
No benefit is automatic. Rates, fees, requirements, and service quality vary. Review current disclosures before opening a Membership or
applying for a loan
.
How Rates and Fees Affect Your Money
Small pricing differences can become expensive over time. A higher Annual Percentage Yield can help your savings grow. A lower Annual Percentage Rate can reduce the cost of borrowing.
Credit unions
often return value to Members through competitive savings yields and reasonable borrowing rates. Banks may offer:
Introductory bonuses
Premium services
Rewards for maintaining larger balances
Online banks may also provide attractive savings yields because they have fewer physical locations.
Look beyond advertised offers. Review the complete fee schedule for each institution.
Common charges may include:
Monthly maintenance fees
Overdraft fees
Out-of-network ATM charges
Wire transfer fees
Minimum balance charges
Early certificate withdrawal penalties
Excess transaction fees
The best account is not always the one with the highest advertised rate. A strong offer can lose value when it includes:
High fees
Strict balance requirements
Limited access
Calculate the likely cost based on how you manage your money. Someone who often uses ATMs will have unique needs from someone who completes every transaction online.
How Service and Financial Guidance Compare
Large banks often use standardized systems that make routine transactions fast. Their size can also support:
National branch networks
Extensive call centers
Broad product selections
Credit unions often place more emphasis on long-term Member relationships. Personal guidance can matter when you are:
Building credit
Buying a vehicle
Preparing for a home purchase
Handling an unexpected expense
A local credit union may also understand Austin housing costs, transportation needs, employment trends, and business conditions. Local knowledge can make financial conversations feel more relevant.

financial well-being

Good guidance should support your research, not replace it. Read all disclosures and compare the total cost of every financial product.
Which Choice Supports Financial Security?
Strong financial security options go beyond deposit insurance. Look for:
Multi-factor authentication
Card-lock tools
Transaction alerts
Identity verification
Secure messaging
Clear fraud-reporting procedures
Federally insured financial institutions are regulated and examined. You can confirm a credit union's insurance status through the NCUA. You can also review how account ownership categories affect your coverage.
UFCU provides information explaining how eligible Member funds are protected.

Frequently Asked Questions
Can You Use Both a Credit Union and a Bank?
Yes. You can use each institution for a different financial goal. You might keep everyday checking and an
auto loan
at a credit union while using an online bank for a promotional savings rate.
Multiple relationships can improve flexibility, but they require organization. Track the following:
Direct deposits
Automatic payments
Minimum balances
Insurance limits
Inactive-account fees
Avoid opening so many accounts that fraud monitoring, tax records, or missed payments become difficult to manage. Setting reminders or using financial apps can help you stay organized. Regularly reviewing your accounts can also prevent unnecessary fees or missed opportunities.
How Do Shared Branches and ATM Networks Work?
Participating credit unions may allow Members to complete basic transactions at another cooperative location. Large ATM networks may also provide surcharge-free withdrawals.
Available services vary between locations. Confirm deposit limits, identification requirements, operating hours, and supported transactions before visiting. Mobile deposits and digital payments can also reduce the need for branch visits when you travel outside Austin.
Checking your credit union's website or app can help you find nearby shared branches quickly. Planning ahead can save time and prevent inconvenience during urgent transactions.

Growing companies should also ask about:
Lines of credit

Payroll tools
Local guidance can be valuable when revenue changes, hiring increases, or expansion requires careful planning.
