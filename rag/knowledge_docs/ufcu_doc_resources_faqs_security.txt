Security FAQs
General Questions About Scams
Is there a difference between a scam and fraud?
Yes. A
scam
potentially occurs when someone receives a suspicious message or is being encouraged to take a suspicious action. If this happens to you, please call
(512) 467-8080
immediately to report the incident.
Fraud
is when an activity has
already
occurred. If you see a transaction you do not recognize, please call
(866) 928-5450
immediately to report the incident and dispute the transaction.
Am I in trouble if I fell for a scam?
No. Scams are designed to trick people. You’re not alone — contact UFCU as soon as possible so we can help you take the next step.
How can I tell if something is a scam?
If it feels urgent, unexpected, or asks for money or personal information, it may be a scam. When in doubt, pause and
verify using a trusted source
. Go directly to the company’s official website or phone number. Never use links or contact information from a suspicious message.
Can scammers really make it look like they’re from UFCU?
Yes. Phone numbers, emails, and websites can be spoofed to look legitimate.
Would UFCU ever ask for my banking information?
No. UFCU will never call, text, or email you asking for your password, PIN, or one-time passcode.
Would UFCU ever ask me to move money to protect my account?
No. Requests to move money to a “safe account” are a common scam tactic.
What should I do if I’m not sure if something is a scam?
Pause, don’t respond, and contact UFCU directly using a verified number.
Who should I contact first if something feels off?
Start with UFCU. Any concern is worth reporting.
Is it okay to report something even if I’m not sure it’s a scam?
Yes. You don’t need certainty to reach out. Reporting early helps protect you and others.
Can scams still look real even if something feels off?
Yes. Scammers often build trust or mimic real organizations to make their messages believable.
What should I never share?
Never share your password, PIN, or one-time passcode with anyone.
What should I do if I feel pressured or rushed?
Pause. Scammers rely on urgency — taking a moment to verify can help protect you.
Verify using a trusted source.
Go directly to the company’s official website or phone number. Never use links or contact information from a suspicious message.
Phishing, Suspicious Messages & Impersonation
What should I do if I receive a message or link that looks real but I’m not sure?
This is often phishing. Scammers send emails, texts, or links that look legitimate but are designed to steal your information.
Don’t click unexpected links.
Go directly to the company’s website yourself. If you already entered information, change your credentials immediately.
What should I do if I receive an email that looks like it’s from my bank, vendor, or team?
These can be phishing or social engineering attempts. Avoid clicking links or downloading attachments. Visit the company’s official website or system directly to confirm. Never rely on contact information from a suspicious message.
Relationship Based Scams
What should I do if someone I’ve built a relationship with asks me for money?
This is a common tactic in romance or friendship scams. Scammers build trust before asking for financial help. Stop communication, do not send money, and only send funds to people you know in real life. Pause and verify using a trusted source.
Threats, Urgency & Pressure
What should I do if someone threatens me with legal action or urgent consequences?
Scammers use fear to push quick decisions. Don’t send money or provide information. End the interaction and look up the agency independently using verified contact information.
Unauthorized Charges, Account Activity, and Disputes
What should I do if my card or account is being used without my authorization?
Review your account activity immediately and lock or secure your card if possible. To start a claim or dispute, call
(866) 928-5450
.
How do I start a fraud claim or dispute?
Call
(866) 928-5450
to review your account and begin the process.
Can UFCU reverse a payment I already sent?
Some payments, like Zelle
®
or other peer-to-peer transfers, may be treated like cash and are not always reversible. Contact UFCU right away to review your situation.
What should I do if I notice unusual transactions or payment activity?
Unauthorized activity can escalate quickly. Review recent transactions, stop using compromised cards or systems, and call
(866) 928-5450
to start a claim or dispute.
Payment Changes, Invoices & Business Related Fraud
What should I do if someone requests a payment change or urgent invoice update?
Invoice scams often redirect payments to fraudulent accounts. Pause and do not process the payment. Verify the request using a known, trusted contact and confirm details using your own records — not the suspicious message.
What should I do if a card, check, or business mail is missing or intercepted?
Stolen items can be used for unauthorized transactions. Report the issue, stop using affected payment methods, request replacements, and monitor your accounts closely.
Data Breaches & System Security
What should I do if I experience a data breach or system compromise?
Stolen credentials or data can be used for ongoing fraud. Update your passwords, secure access immediately, review your systems, and limit access until everything is confirmed safe.
